My team was able to deliver a software inventory solution for the bank within 6 months. It may sound as a simple statement but when I go evolved into the project:

- The project has been running for 2 years and was overdue

- There was a regulatory commitment to finish it within 5 months

- The team that worked on the project for the last 2 years left

- The budget was overspent

After I got my hands on the project the next 6 months became a marathon. I have met daily with the team to discuss the progress and with stakeholders to discuss the requirements. On thing I have found out was that during the past 2 years the project grew too big because everyone wanted some extra features.

While the engineering team was working on building the architecture design, I was working on removing the scope creep. I went through myriad of daily calls running a wave after wave of descoping the unnecessary features.

Once we were happy with the list of requirements and start building, we have run into an issue that the infrastructure purchased could not cope with the load that the application was accumulating and option to acquire a new hardware was not available to us.

I have split the team into smaller groups and each group started prototyping trying to find a solution that could accumulate the load. We have gone through 8 prototypes before we have found a suitable one and it still required some extra funds. I had to secure the funds and it was not an easy task.

In the end we were late 1 moth for an internal audit but were on time for an external one and have successfully passed it.

I was able to take ownership of a completely ruined project, find people capable of delivering it, rebuild the design, rebuild the scope and deliver it within very tight schedule.
